# Levinson, *The Box: How the Shipping Container Made the World Smaller and the World Economy Bigger* (Princeton University Press, 2nd ed., 2016)

Bibliographic record (verbatim from the publisher's page):

- Title: "The Box: How the Shipping Container Made the World Smaller and the World Economy Bigger - Second Edition with a new chapter by the author"
- Author: Marc Levinson
- Publisher: Princeton University Press; Second Edition, April 5, 2016
- ISBN: 9780691170817

Publisher's description (verbatim excerpts):

"In April 1956, a refitted oil tanker carried fifty-eight shipping containers from Newark to Houston. From that modest beginning, container shipping developed into a huge industry that made the boom in global trade possible."

The description credits the "delicate negotiations on standards that made it possible for almost any container to travel on any truck or train or ship."

It describes Malcolm McLean as "an iconoclastic entrepreneur" whose "drive and imagination turned containerization from an impractical idea into a phenomenon that transformed economic geography."

The container "paved the way for Asia to become the world's workshop and brought consumers a previously unimaginable variety of low-cost products from around the globe."

What this supports in the book: that the gain from containerization came from standardizing the container so that any carrier could handle any box (Ch 1 The Voyage Ahead; Ch 2 §8 and epigraph). It does NOT support specific engineering claims about corner-fitting geometry or ISO 668 / ISO 1161 dimensions; keep those at the level of "standardized dimensions and fittings" or declare them as the usual telling. The McLean-released-the-patents claim remains unverified and must not be drafted.
